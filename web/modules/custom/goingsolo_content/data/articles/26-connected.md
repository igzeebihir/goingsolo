Your phone is your map, tour guide, taxi rank and link to home when you travel alone. A little setup before you go saves trouble later.

## Mobile coverage

Coverage is good in towns and along main roads, but it can be patchy in mountains, glens and on remote peninsulas and islands. Don't rely on your phone alone for navigation on hill walks.

## Data

- **EU and EEA visitors** can usually use their home allowance in Ireland under EU roaming rules, subject to fair use limits.
- **Visitors from the UK** should check with their provider, as roaming terms vary since Brexit.
- **Visitors from further away** can buy an Irish prepaid SIM or eSIM from operators such as Three, Vodafone or Eir, or use a travel eSIM.

Northern Ireland is part of the UK network, so check your plan covers both if you'll cross the border.

## Wi-Fi

Free Wi-Fi is common in hotels, cafés, pubs, libraries and on intercity trains and coaches. Speeds vary in rural areas.

## Useful apps

- **TFI Journey Planner** and **TFI Live**: public transport times and live departures in the Republic.
- **Translink**: buses and trains in Northern Ireland.
- **Irish Rail**: train tickets and times.
- **FreeNow** and **Uber**: taxis in cities.
- **Met Éireann**: Ireland's national weather forecasts and warnings.
- **Offline maps**: download the area you're visiting in Google Maps or a hiking app before you go.

## Battery

Carry a small power bank, especially on long days out. Cold, wet weather drains batteries quickly.

## Let someone know

Share your live location with a friend or family member, or at least send them your daily plan and where you're staying. It's the simplest safety habit for travelling alone.
