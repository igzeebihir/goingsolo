Donegal has some of the most dramatic scenery in Ireland and far fewer visitors than Kerry or Clare. For a solo traveller that means quiet beaches, empty roads and a warm welcome in small towns.

## Highlights

- **Sliabh Liag (Slieve League)**, among the highest sea cliffs in Europe. The visitor centre runs a shuttle, and you can walk up to the viewing point.
- **Glenveagh National Park and Castle**, with lakeside walks and gardens. A shuttle bus runs from the visitor centre to the castle.
- **Fanad Head lighthouse**, on a peninsula between Lough Swilly and Mulroy Bay.
- **Malin Head**, the most northerly point on the island of Ireland.
- **Beaches** such as Portsalon, Marble Hill and Narin.

## Gaeltacht areas

Parts of Donegal are Gaeltacht, where Irish is the community language. Gweedore and the surrounding area are known for music, and you'll see road signs in Irish only.

## Getting around

Donegal has no railway. Buses connect Letterkenny and Donegal town with Dublin, Derry, Sligo and Galway, but rural services are limited. Most visitors drive. If you'd rather not, consider a guided tour: some UK operators run Donegal and Wild Atlantic Way tours designed specifically for solo travellers, with no single supplement.

## Bases

**Donegal town** is a good start for the south of the county and Sliabh Liag. **Letterkenny** is the largest town, with plenty of hotels and good bus links. **Dunfanaghy** is a small, attractive village near Horn Head and Ards Forest Park.

## Weather and light

Summer days are very long this far north, with light until late in the evening. Winter is short-dayed and stormy, but dramatic. Bring waterproofs in any season.
