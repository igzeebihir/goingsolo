Ireland isn't a cheap country to travel in, and travelling alone means you can't split the cost of a room, a taxi or a rental car. But with a bit of planning a solo trip can be good value.

## Accommodation: the biggest cost

Your bed will be the largest part of the bill. To keep it down:

- Look for [solo packages and single rooms](/deals) rather than paying for a double.
- Mix it up: a comfortable hotel for a couple of nights, then a guesthouse or a private hostel room.
- Stay two or three nights in one place. Many hotels discount longer stays, and you save on travel days.
- Avoid big event weekends in cities.

## Getting around

Public transport is far cheaper than renting a car alone. Intercity trains and buses connect most cities and large towns, and advance online fares on Irish Rail are usually cheaper than buying at the station. If you're 66 or over and live in Ireland, the Free Travel Pass covers most public transport. See our guide to [getting around without a car](/guides/getting-around-ireland-without-a-car).

Day tours from Dublin, Galway, Cork and Killarney are good value for one person, because you'd otherwise pay for a car, fuel and parking.

## Eating

- A hotel breakfast set you up for a long day and lets you have a lighter lunch.
- Many pubs serve good food at lunchtime for less than evening restaurant prices.
- Early bird menus, typically served before 7pm, are common and much cheaper than à la carte.
- Supermarkets and bakeries make for an easy picnic on a walking day.

## Free things to do

Many of Ireland's national museums and galleries are free, including the National Gallery, the National Museum sites and the Chester Beatty in Dublin. Most beaches, forest parks, greenways and walking trails cost nothing. Traditional music sessions in pubs are free to listen to.

## Keep a small buffer

Weather changes plans in Ireland. Leave a little room in the budget for a taxi on a wet evening or an extra night somewhere you love.
