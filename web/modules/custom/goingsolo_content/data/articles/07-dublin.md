Dublin is compact, friendly and well connected, which makes it one of the easiest places in Ireland to travel alone. Most of the centre is walkable, the DART runs along the coast, and many of the best museums are free.

## Day one: the city centre

Start at Trinity College and the Book of Kells (book a time slot in advance). Walk down Grafton Street to St Stephen's Green, then on to Merrion Square with its Georgian doors. The National Gallery and the National Museum of Ireland on Kildare Street are free and close together, so you can dip in and out.

In the evening, head for a traditional music session. Temple Bar is busy and expensive; pubs a few streets away often have better sessions and are easier for a solo visitor to settle into.

## Day two: history and the Liffey

The Chester Beatty in the grounds of Dublin Castle is one of Ireland's finest museums and free to enter. From there walk to Christ Church Cathedral and St Patrick's Cathedral. Cross the river to the EPIC emigration museum or the Jeanie Johnston famine ship in the Docklands.

Kilmainham Gaol is worth the trip west of the centre, but tickets sell out, so book ahead.

## Day three: the coast by DART

Take the DART north to Howth for the cliff walk and fish and chips by the harbour, or south to Dalkey and Killiney for views over the bay. Both are easy half-days, and you'll be back in town for dinner.

## Where to stay

Dublin is expensive, especially at weekends. Look for hotels with true single rooms, or try a modern hotel with compact rooms priced for one. Sunday to Thursday is usually cheaper. Check our [Dublin deals](/county/dublin) for current solo offers.

## Eating alone

Dublin has plenty of counter seating, food halls and cafés where eating alone is completely normal. Many restaurants offer early bird menus before 7pm, which are good value. Our guide to [eating alone in Ireland](/guides/eating-alone-in-ireland-restaurants-and-pubs-that-welcome-solo-diners) has more tips.

## Safety

Dublin is generally safe, but like any city it has pickpockets in busy areas and some streets that feel rough late at night. Stick to busy, well-lit streets after dark and use licensed taxis or the FreeNow app.
