Online booking engines are built for couples and families. The price you see is usually for a double room, and there's rarely a box for "just me". The good news is that Irish hotels, especially independent ones, are often happy to quote a lower rate for one person if you ask the right way.

## Call, don't click

Ring the hotel directly instead of using a booking site. Reception staff can see how full the hotel is and often have more room to move on price than the website shows. Booking direct also means the hotel doesn't pay commission, which makes a discount easier for them.

## When to call

Call mid-morning on a weekday, when reception is quieter. Avoid check-in time (around 3pm to 6pm) and weekends.

## A simple script

> Hi, I'm hoping to stay on the 12th and 13th of March. I'll be travelling on my own. Do you have a single room, or a rate for single occupancy of a double?

If they quote the full double rate:

> Is there any flexibility for one person? I'm flexible on dates if a midweek night would be easier.

If there's a solo package:

> Could you tell me what's included and whether there's a supplement on top?

## What helps

- **Be flexible.** Offering two or three possible dates gives the hotel a chance to fill a quiet night.
- **Stay longer.** A two- or three-night stay is worth more to a hotel than one night, and makes a discount more likely.
- **Ask about dinner, bed and breakfast.** Hotels often have more margin in dinner packages and may drop the supplement to sell one.
- **Be friendly and brief.** The person answering the phone is the one who can help you.

## If the answer is still no
