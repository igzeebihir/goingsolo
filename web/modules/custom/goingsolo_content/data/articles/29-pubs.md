The pub is the heart of social life in much of Ireland, and walking in on your own is completely normal. Here's how to feel at home.

## Choosing a pub

Look for smaller, traditional pubs a street or two away from the main tourist strip. They're often cheaper, friendlier and have better music. Ask your hotel or B&B where locals go.

## Where to sit

Sit at the bar if you'd like conversation. Bar staff are often great hosts and will introduce you to regulars. If you'd rather keep to yourself, a small table or a snug works fine.

## Ordering

There's no table service in most pubs: order at the bar. You don't need to tip for drinks, but leaving the change or a euro or two is appreciated. If you're not drinking alcohol, soft drinks, alcohol-free beers and good coffee are widely available, and nobody will mind.

## Rounds

Irish groups buy drinks in rounds, with each person taking a turn. If someone buys you a drink, it's polite to buy one back. You can always say "I'm grand for now, thanks".

## Music sessions

At a traditional session, musicians sit together and play tunes. It isn't a performance, so you don't clap after every tune, and you shouldn't ask them to play requests. Find a seat nearby, keep conversation down while they play, and enjoy it. Sessions often start around 9pm.

## Food

Many pubs serve food during the day and early evening. A bowl of seafood chowder with brown bread is a classic.

## Closing time

Pubs generally close around 11.30pm on weeknights and later at weekends. When the bar staff call "time", finish up.
