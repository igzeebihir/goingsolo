Travel insurance isn't the most exciting part of planning a trip, but for a solo traveller it matters more. If you fall ill or have an accident, there's no companion to handle phone calls, rebook transport or get you home. A decent policy fills that gap.

This guide is general information, not financial advice. Read your policy wording carefully and ask the insurer if anything is unclear.

## If you live in Ireland and are holidaying at home

Many people assume insurance is only for trips abroad. For a holiday in Ireland it's still worth checking:

- **Cancellation cover.** If you have to cancel a non-refundable hotel package or tour because of illness, can you claim the cost back?
- **Your existing policies.** Some home insurance and credit cards include limited travel cover, including domestic trips. Check before you buy something new.

## If you're visiting Ireland from abroad

- **Medical cover.** EU visitors can use the European Health Insurance Card (EHIC) for necessary public healthcare. It doesn't cover private care or getting you home. Visitors from the UK can use the GHIC. Visitors from the US, Canada and elsewhere need private cover.
- **Repatriation.** Check the policy covers bringing you home if you're too ill to travel normally.

## Things that matter more when you're alone

- **24-hour assistance line.** You want someone who will help organise things, not just reimburse you later.
- **Cover for a friend or relative to travel to you** if you're hospitalised.
- **Activities.** If you're hiking, cycling, sea swimming or kayaking, make sure the policy covers it.
- **Age limits.** Some annual policies have upper age limits or higher premiums for older travellers. Specialist insurers cover older travellers well.

## Keep details handy

Save your policy number and the assistance phone number on your phone and on paper. Share your itinerary with someone at home, and tell them which insurer you're with.
