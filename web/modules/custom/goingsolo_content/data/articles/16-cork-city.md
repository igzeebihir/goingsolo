Cork is a friendly, walkable city built on islands in the River Lee. It has a strong food culture, good galleries and easy rail connections to Cobh, Fota and the rest of the country.

## The English Market

This covered food market dates back to the 18th century and is the heart of the city. Browse the stalls, then take a seat upstairs at the Farmgate Café, which overlooks the market floor and is easy for solo diners.

## Galleries and culture

- **Crawford Art Gallery**, free and right in the centre.
- **The Glucksman** on the University College Cork campus, a striking modern gallery.
- **Shandon**, where you can ring the bells of St Anne's Church.
- **Cork City Gaol**, a short walk west of the centre.

## Day trips by train

Commuter trains from Kent Station reach **Cobh** in about 25 minutes. Cobh was the last port of call of the Titanic and the departure point for millions of emigrants; the Titanic Experience and Cobh Heritage Centre tell those stories. Trains also stop at **Fota**, for the wildlife park and Fota House.

**Kinsale** and **Blarney Castle** are reached by bus.

## Evenings

Cork has a good pub scene, with live music and traditional sessions, and plenty of restaurants offering early bird menus. The city centre is busy until late at weekends.
