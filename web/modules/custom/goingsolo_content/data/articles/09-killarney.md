Killarney is one of Ireland's most visited towns, and that works in a solo traveller's favour. It has a train station, plenty of hotels in every price range, and dozens of tours that start from the town centre. Killarney National Park begins at the edge of town.

## Getting there

Trains to Killarney run from Dublin Heuston, usually with a change at Mallow, and from Cork. Buses connect Killarney with Cork, Limerick, Tralee and Kerry Airport. The station is right in town.

## Killarney National Park on foot or by bike

Rent a bike in town and cycle the flat paths to Ross Castle and around Muckross. Muckross House and its gardens, Torc Waterfall and the shores of the lakes are all within easy reach. Jaunting cars, the horse-drawn carts at the edge of town, are an option if you'd rather sit back.

## The Ring of Kerry by coach

Day tours of the Ring of Kerry leave Killarney every morning in season. You'll see Kenmare Bay, Waterville and the Kerry coast without the stress of driving narrow roads with tour buses coming the other way. It's a long day, so bring snacks.

## The Gap of Dunloe

A classic Killarney day: a bus or jaunting car to Kate Kearney's Cottage, a walk or pony ride through the Gap of Dunloe, then a boat back across the lakes to Ross Castle. Several local operators package this together.

## Evenings

Killarney's pubs have music most nights in season, and the town is busy and well lit in the evenings, which many solo travellers find reassuring.
