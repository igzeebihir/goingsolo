Galway has a reputation as Ireland's most welcoming city, and for solo travellers it lives up to it. The centre is small enough to walk in twenty minutes, there's music in the pubs every night of the week, and it's the natural base for Connemara and the Aran Islands.

## Getting there

Trains run from Dublin Heuston in about two and a half hours, and direct coaches link Galway with Dublin, Dublin Airport and Shannon Airport. You don't need a car to enjoy the city.

## What to do

- **Walk the Latin Quarter.** Shop Street, Quay Street and the lanes around them are full of buskers, bookshops and cafés.
- **The Spanish Arch and the Long Walk.** Watch the River Corrib meet Galway Bay, then cross to the Claddagh.
- **Walk the Prom at Salthill.** It's tradition to kick the wall at the far end. Hardy locals swim at Blackrock diving tower all year.
- **Galway City Museum** is free and has good exhibitions on the city's history.
- **The Saturday market** by St Nicholas' Church is great for lunch on the go.

## Music

Traditional sessions happen in many pubs every evening. Sitting at the bar with a drink and listening is completely normal for someone on their own, and conversation tends to follow. Arrive early for a good spot.

## Day trips

Day tours run from Galway to Connemara and Kylemore Abbey, the Cliffs of Moher and the Burren. Ferries to Inis Mór, the largest Aran Island, leave from Rossaveal, with a connecting bus from the city. These tours suit solo travellers well because you get company and no driving.
