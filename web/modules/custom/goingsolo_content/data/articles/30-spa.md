Spa hotels are full of couples at weekends, but a solo spa break can be one of the most restorative ways to travel. No need to compromise on treatments, no small talk if you don't want it, and time to properly switch off.

## Look for solo packages

A growing number of Irish spa hotels now offer packages for one, often including a treatment, breakfast and dinner. They're usually much better value than booking a double room and adding treatments. Cashel Palace and The Brehon in Killarney are two examples; see all current offers on our [deals page](/deals) and filter by "Spa or wellness".

## Go midweek

Spas are quieter Sunday to Thursday, which means more choice of treatment times and a calmer relaxation room. Prices are often lower too.

## What to check

- **Is spa access included?** Some packages include the thermal suite or pool; others only the room.
- **Book treatments early.** The best time slots go first, especially on weekends.
- **Dinner.** A solo package that includes dinner saves the decision later. Ask for a table with a view.
- **Location.** A spa near walks, the sea or a park gives you something to do between treatments.

## Uniquely Irish treatments

- **Seaweed baths** are a west coast tradition, especially in Sligo, Mayo, Clare and Kerry. You soak in hot seawater with fresh seaweed.
- **Thermal pools by the sea**, often with sauna and cold plunge, have opened in several coastal towns.

## Day spas

If an overnight stay isn't in the budget, many hotels sell day spa packages with a treatment, lunch and access to the pool and thermal suite.
