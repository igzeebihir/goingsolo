Sea swimming is now a year-round passion for many people in Ireland, and you'll see swimmers at piers, coves and beaches around the coast on even the coldest mornings. It's a wonderful thing to try on a solo trip, but the Irish sea is cold and can be dangerous.

This is general information. If you're not a confident swimmer, start with a lifeguarded beach in summer.

## Golden rules

- **Never swim alone if you can avoid it.** Join others at a popular swimming spot, where regulars are usually happy to have company.
- **Swim at lifeguarded beaches** during the summer season where possible.
- **Know the water temperature.** Even in summer the Irish sea rarely gets above the mid-teens Celsius. Cold water shock is a real risk: enter slowly, and get out before you feel very cold.
- **Check conditions.** Look at tides, wind and swell. Don't swim in big waves or where there are rip currents or warning signs.
- **Stay within your depth** and close to shore.
- **Wear a bright swim hat and use a tow float** so you can be seen.
- **Have warm clothes and a hot drink ready** for afterwards.

## Popular spots

- **The Forty Foot**, Sandycove, Dublin: a famous year-round bathing place.
- **Blackrock diving tower**, Salthill, Galway.
- **Vico Baths**, Dalkey, Dublin.
- **Guillamene Cove**, Tramore, Waterford.
- **Seapoint**, Dublin, a sheltered spot popular with beginners.

## In an emergency

Call 112 or 999 and ask for the Coast Guard. If you get into trouble, float on your back to catch your breath, then signal for help.

## Water safety advice

Water Safety Ireland publishes beach safety information and lists of lifeguarded beaches each summer.
