A single supplement is the extra charge a hotel or tour operator adds when one person uses a room priced for two. Irish hotel deals are very often advertised "per person sharing", so the headline price assumes two people. Book alone and you may be asked to pay for the empty half of the room, or a supplement on top of the per-person price.

The reasoning is simple: the room costs the hotel roughly the same to clean, heat and staff whether one or two people sleep in it. But for solo travellers the result can be a bill 30% to 100% higher than the advertised rate.

## Ways to pay less

**Look for real single rooms.** Older hotels, city hotels and some B&Bs still have single rooms. They're smaller, but they're priced for one. Ask for them by name, because they're rarely shown first on booking sites.

**Look for solo packages.** A growing number of Irish hotels now run packages designed for one person, often with dinner or spa time included. Cashel Palace, The Maritime in Bantry and The Brehon in Killarney all have one. You'll find them gathered on our [deals page](/deals).

**Travel midweek and off-season.** Hotels are far more willing to give one person a fair rate on a Tuesday in November than a Saturday in August. Sunday to Thursday is the sweet spot.

**Ask.** Ring the hotel and ask: "Do you have a rate for one person?" Many will quote a single occupancy price lower than the double rate, especially if the hotel isn't full. Our guide on [how to ask an Irish hotel for a solo rate](/guides/how-to-ask-an-irish-hotel-for-a-solo-rate) has a script you can use.

**Choose tours that match you with a roommate.** Some walking and tour companies will pair you with another solo guest of the same gender, so you pay the shared price. If no match is found, some waive the supplement anyway.

**Consider self-catering or hostels.** A studio apartment or a private room in a good hostel is priced per unit, not per person, so there's nothing to supplement.

## When a supplement is fair

Not every supplement is a rip-off. A small country hotel with ten rooms genuinely loses money if one person takes a double on a busy weekend. A modest supplement, clearly shown, is reasonable.
