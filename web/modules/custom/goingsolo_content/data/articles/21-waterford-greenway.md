The Waterford Greenway is one of the best ways to spend a day or two on a bike in Ireland, and it's ideal for solo travellers. It's flat, traffic-free and well signed, so you can go at your own pace without worrying about navigation or cars.

## The route

The greenway follows the old Waterford to Mallow railway line for about 46km between **Waterford city** and **Dungarvan**. Along the way it crosses the Kilmacthomas viaduct and the Durrow viaduct, passes through the Ballyvoile tunnel and runs beside the River Suir and the Comeragh Mountains before reaching the sea.

## Doing it alone

Bike hire companies at both ends and along the way rent bikes, including e-bikes, and most offer a shuttle back to your starting point. That means you can ride one way and enjoy it without worrying about the return.

Many riders split it: Waterford to Kilmacthomas (about 23km) and Kilmacthomas to Dungarvan (about 23km). Kilmacthomas has a café at the old Workhouse, a good halfway stop.

## Where to stay

- **Waterford city** for history: the Viking Triangle museums and House of Waterford Crystal.
- **Dungarvan** for the sea, good restaurants and a pretty harbour.

Stay a night at each end if you want a relaxed two-day trip.

## Other greenways

If you enjoy it, Ireland has several more: the Great Western Greenway in Mayo, the Limerick Greenway, the Old Rail Trail between Athlone and Mullingar, and the Royal Canal Greenway across the midlands.
