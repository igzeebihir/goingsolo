Many women travel around Ireland alone every year and find it relaxed and welcoming. The country is used to solo visitors, people are chatty, and there's a strong culture of looking out for strangers. As anywhere, a little planning helps.

## Where to stay

- **Choose central accommodation** in towns and cities, so you can walk back after dinner on busy streets.
- **Small hotels and B&Bs** are often run by families who will happily give you advice, call you a taxi and keep an eye out.
- **Women-only options** exist on some walking and adventure tours. Some hostels have female-only dorms.
- Read recent reviews, especially from other solo women.

## Pubs and evenings

Irish pubs are social places and it's normal to go in alone. You'll often be drawn into conversation, especially at a music session. Most of the time it's simply friendliness. If attention is unwanted, a clear "I'm grand, thanks" usually ends it, and bar staff will step in if needed.

Keep an eye on your drink, as you would anywhere.

## Getting around

- Public transport is safe and widely used by women travelling alone.
- Use app-booked or official taxis at night.
- If you're driving, plan routes in daylight where possible, as rural roads can be very dark and signal can drop out.

## Group tours and activities

Small-group day tours, walking holidays and retreats are a good way to see more of Ireland without being on your own all the time. Several companies run women-only departures.

## If something goes wrong

Call 112 or 999. Gardaí (police) are generally approachable and helpful. The Rape Crisis Help line (1800 77 8888 in the Republic) and Women's Aid (1800 341 900) offer free 24-hour support.
