Winter is underrated as a time to travel alone in Ireland. Hotels are quiet and keen for guests, solo deals are at their most generous, and there's something wonderful about a bracing coastal walk followed by a fire and a good dinner.

## What to expect

- **Short days.** In December it's light from about 8.30am to 4.30pm. Plan your outdoor time for the middle of the day.
- **Mixed weather.** Expect wind and rain, but also crisp, bright days. Pack waterproofs and warm layers.
- **Seasonal closures.** Some attractions, ferries, cafés and B&Bs close or reduce their hours, particularly on the west coast and islands. Check before you go.

## Ideas for a winter break

- **A city break** in Dublin, Belfast, Cork, Galway or Kilkenny, with museums, galleries, theatre and pubs to keep you warm.
- **A country house hotel** with fires, long dinners and walks on the estate.
- **A spa break**, ideally midweek, when you'll have the pool to yourself.
- **Winter sea swimming**, joining the hardy locals at a popular spot (read our [sea swimming guide](/guides/sea-swimming-in-ireland-for-solo-swimmers) first).
- **The winter solstice at Newgrange**, where a lottery decides who gets to stand inside the passage tomb at sunrise. Anyone can visit the site at other times.
- **Christmas markets** in late November and December.

## Getting the best price

Mid-November to mid-December and January to mid-March are the quietest periods. Many hotels run solo packages and midweek specials at these times. See current offers on our [deals page](/deals).

## Driving in winter

Roads can be icy on clear nights, especially in the midlands and uplands. Allow extra time, and avoid long drives after dark if you can.
