Sligo packs a lot into a small area: a lively county town, Benbulben's table-top mountain, surf beaches and the landscapes that inspired W.B. Yeats. Trains run direct from Dublin Connolly to Sligo, so it's easy to reach without a car.

## Sligo town

The town has a good range of cafés and pubs, the Model arts centre, and the Yeats Building. It's a convenient base, with buses out to Strandhill and Rosses Point.

## Strandhill

A surf village about 15 minutes from town, with a long beach (not safe for swimming because of strong currents), surf schools for all levels, and Voya seaweed baths, a uniquely Irish way to warm up after a walk. Knocknarea, the hill above the village, has a cairn said to be Queen Maeve's grave and a well-made path to the top.

## Benbulben and Drumcliffe

Benbulben dominates the north of the county. Yeats is buried in the churchyard at Drumcliffe, beneath it. The Benbulben Forest Walk is a well-marked loop.

## Mullaghmore

A pretty harbour village with a long sandy beach and views of Classiebawn Castle. It's popular with surfers in winter when big waves arrive offshore.

## Lough Gill

The lake east of town holds the Lake Isle of Innisfree. Boat trips run in summer.

Sligo town and Strandhill have hotels, guesthouses and a lively hostel scene. Some solo tours for travellers from Britain base themselves at Mullaghmore. See our [Sligo deals](/county/sligo).
