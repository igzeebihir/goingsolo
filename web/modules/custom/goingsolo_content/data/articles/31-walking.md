Walking is one of the best ways to see Ireland, and it suits solo travellers perfectly. You set your own pace, meet people on the trail, and the country is full of well-marked routes.

## Three ways to do it

**Guided small-group walking holidays.** A guide leads the walks, accommodation and transport are arranged, and you walk with a small group. This is the most sociable option and the safest in the mountains. Some companies match solo travellers with a same-gender roommate so there's no supplement.

**Self-guided walking holidays.** A company books your B&Bs, moves your luggage each day and gives you maps and route notes, but you walk alone. Popular routes include the Wicklow Way, the Kerry Way, the Dingle Way and the Burren Way. Ask about single room supplements, which vary a lot between companies.

**A base and day walks.** Pick a town such as Killarney, Westport, Clifden or Newcastle in County Down, and do day walks from there. Local walking festivals and guided walks add company.

## Great walking areas

- **Killarney National Park and the Kerry Way.**
- **The Wicklow Mountains and the Wicklow Way.**
- **Connemara and the Burren.**
- **The Mourne Mountains** in County Down.
- **The Sheep's Head and Beara peninsulas** in West Cork.

## Safety on your own

- Stick to waymarked trails if you're not an experienced hill walker.
- Check the Met Éireann mountain forecast and leave word of your route.
- Carry waterproofs, layers, food, water and a charged phone.
- In mist, turn back rather than push on.
- In an emergency in the mountains, call 112 or 999 and ask for Mountain Rescue.

## When to go

May, June and September are often the best months: long days, fewer midges and less crowding than July and August.
