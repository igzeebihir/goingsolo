A festival is one of the easiest ways to travel alone. There's a built-in reason to talk to strangers, a programme to fill your days, and a buzz in town. Book accommodation early, as towns fill up fast. Dates change each year, so check festival websites.

## Spring

- **St Patrick's Festival**, Dublin and nationwide (March). Big, busy and fun, with parades in towns all over the country.
- **Cúirt International Festival of Literature**, Galway (April). Readings and talks in a small, sociable setting.
- **Burren in Bloom and the Burren walking events** (May), when the wildflowers are at their best.

## Summer

- **Cat Laughs Comedy Festival**, Kilkenny (early June).
- **Bloomsday**, Dublin (16 June). Readings and walks following Leopold Bloom's route through *Ulysses*.
- **Galway International Arts Festival** (July). Theatre, art and street performance across the city.
- **Willie Clancy Summer School**, Miltown Malbay, Clare (July). A week of traditional music, with sessions in every pub.
- **Kilkenny Arts Festival** (August).
- **Fleadh Cheoil na hÉireann** (August), the biggest festival of Irish traditional music, held in a different town most years.

## Autumn

- **Galway International Oyster and Seafood Festival** (September).
- **Dublin Theatre Festival** (late September to October).
- **Wexford Festival Opera** (October to November).
- **Cork Jazz Festival** (October bank holiday weekend).

## Winter

- **TradFest**, Dublin (January), with concerts in historic venues.
- **Christmas markets** in Belfast, Galway, Waterford and other cities.

## Tips for solo festival-goers

- Book accommodation early, or stay in a nearby town with a good bus link.
- Volunteer: many festivals need stewards and helpers, which is a great way to meet people.
- Join free events, walking tours and talks, where it's easy to chat.
