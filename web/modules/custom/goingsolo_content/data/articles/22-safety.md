Ireland is generally a safe place to travel alone. Violent crime against visitors is rare, people are friendly and helpful, and English is spoken everywhere. Most problems solo travellers face are the same as anywhere: petty theft in busy places, the occasional rowdy night out, and the weather.

## In cities

- **Pickpockets** operate in crowded places like Temple Bar in Dublin, on busy buses and at big events. Keep your phone and wallet in a zipped pocket or bag.
- **Late at night**, stick to busy, well-lit streets. Some areas near city-centre bus and train stations can feel rough after dark.
- **Taxis** are safest when booked through an app like FreeNow or Uber, or taken from an official rank. Licensed taxis display a roof sign and a driver ID.

## In the countryside

The main risks are natural, not human:

- **Roads.** Many rural roads are narrow and have no footpath. If you're walking along one, face oncoming traffic and wear something bright.
- **Cliffs and coasts.** Stay behind barriers at cliff tops, which can be undercut, and be careful on wet rocks.
- **Weather in the hills.** Mist and rain can come in fast. Tell someone your route, take a map and a charged phone, and turn back if conditions change.
- **Sea swimming.** Swim at lifeguarded beaches in summer, and read our [sea swimming guide](/guides/sea-swimming-in-ireland-for-solo-swimmers).

## Emergency numbers

Call **112** or **999** for Gardaí (police), ambulance, fire or coast guard, in both the Republic and Northern Ireland.

## Simple habits

- Share your plans and accommodation details with someone at home.
- Keep your phone charged and carry a small power bank.
- Trust your instincts. If a place or person makes you uneasy, leave.
- Keep a copy of your ID and insurance details separate from the originals.
