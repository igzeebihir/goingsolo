Plenty of people who travel alone have a dog at home, and for many the dog is the reason they don't travel at all. More Irish hotels and guesthouses now take pets, and a break with your dog can feel a lot less solo.

## Finding a room

Pet-friendly rooms are usually a small number of ground-floor rooms with outdoor access, so they go first. Book early, book direct and tell the hotel you are bringing a dog when you book, not when you arrive.

Before you book, ask:

- **Is there a charge?** It ranges from nothing to €75 a night. Some hotels hold a deposit instead.
- **What size and how many dogs?** Many set a limit of one or two dogs, often no bigger than a Labrador.
- **Can I leave the dog in the room?** This matters most when you are alone. Some hotels say never, some allow an hour, and some arrange a dog sitter.
- **Where can the dog go?** Bar, lounge, terrace or only your room.

Then ask for the single occupancy rate, as you would for any solo stay. See our [pet-friendly stays](/pet-friendly) for places that have said yes.

## Eating out

Travelling alone with a dog means you can't leave them with a companion while you eat. Hotels that let dogs into the bar or a lounge, or serve dinner at pet-friendly tables, make a solo trip far easier. Plenty of Irish pubs welcome dogs too, especially in rural areas, but always ask first.

## Getting around

A car is by far the easiest option. Trains, buses and ferries have their own pet rules, which often limit dogs to small ones in carriers or to particular seats or cabins. Check with the operator before you travel. Assistance dogs are covered by separate rules.

## Coming from abroad

Dogs coming into Ireland need a microchip, a rabies vaccination and a pet passport or health certificate. Dogs arriving from Great Britain and most non-EU countries also need tapeworm treatment shortly before travel. The rules change, so check the Department of Agriculture's pet travel pages on gov.ie well before you book a ferry.

## Out walking

Keep your dog on a lead around livestock, especially during lambing season in spring. Some walking routes cross farmland where dogs are not allowed at all, and many beaches restrict dogs during summer bathing hours. Read the signs at the start of a walk, and bring bags. Our guide to [dog-friendly walks for a solo break](/guides/dog-friendly-walks-for-a-solo-break) has some good places to start.
