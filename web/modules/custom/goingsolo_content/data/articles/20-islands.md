Ireland's inhabited islands are some of the most peaceful places to travel alone. They're small enough to explore on foot or by bike, and island communities are used to welcoming visitors.

## Inis Mór, Aran Islands (Galway)

The largest Aran Island is reached by ferry from Rossaveal in Connemara, with a connecting bus from Galway city, or by a short flight from Inverin. Hire a bike at the pier and cycle to **Dún Aonghasa**, a prehistoric fort on the edge of a 100-metre cliff. Inis Meáin and Inis Oírr, the smaller islands, are even quieter.

## Inishbofin (Galway)

A ferry from Cleggan near Clifden takes about 30 minutes. Inishbofin has waymarked loop walks, sandy beaches and a strong music tradition, with an arts festival in May.

## Achill Island (Mayo)

Linked to the mainland by a bridge, Achill is the easiest island to reach. It has spectacular beaches such as Keem Bay, the Atlantic Drive and the end of the Great Western Greenway.

## Cape Clear (Cork)

Ireland's most southerly inhabited island is reached by ferry from Baltimore. It's a Gaeltacht island with a bird observatory and lovely cliff walks.

## Arranmore (Donegal)

A short ferry from Burtonport, Arranmore has a lively community, cliff walks and a waymarked loop around the island.

## Practical tips

- **Check ferry timetables**, which are reduced outside summer and can be cancelled in bad weather.
- **Book accommodation ahead**, especially in summer, as islands have limited beds.
- **Bring cash** for small shops and bike hire, as not everywhere takes cards.
- **Consider staying the night.** Islands are at their best after the day visitors leave.
