For many solo travellers, the hardest part of the day is dinner. In Ireland it needn't be. Pubs, cafés and many restaurants are relaxed about solo diners, and you'll often end up chatting to someone.

## Where it's easiest

- **Pubs that serve food.** Sitting at the bar or a small table in a pub is completely normal on your own. Many pubs serve good food all day.
- **Hotel restaurants and bars.** If you're staying in a hotel, its bar is an easy option after a long day, and staff will look after you.
- **Counter and kitchen seating.** More restaurants now have counter seats facing the kitchen, perfect for one.
- **Markets and food halls.** The English Market in Cork, St George's Market in Belfast and weekend food markets around the country are good for lunch.
- **Cafés.** Irish cafés are great for a long breakfast or lunch with a book.

## Tips

- **Book a table for one.** Restaurants won't mind, and it means you won't be squeezed by the door.
- **Eat early.** Early bird menus, usually before 7pm, are cheaper and the room is quieter.
- **Ask for a good table.** A window seat or a spot where you can see the room makes all the difference.
- **Bring something to read** if you like, or don't. People-watching is fine too.
- **Choose a package with dinner.** Several Irish hotels include dinner in their solo packages, which takes the decision away.

## Meeting people over food

Some hotels and guesthouses have communal tables at breakfast or dinner. Cookery classes and food tours are another sociable option, and you'll find them in Dublin, Galway, Cork, Kinsale and Belfast.
