More people than ever are travelling alone in their 60s, 70s and beyond, whether after retirement, bereavement or simply because they want to. Ireland is a particularly easy and welcoming place to do it.

## Free travel

If you live in Ireland and are 66 or over, the Free Travel Pass covers most Irish Rail, Bus Éireann, Dublin Bus, Luas, DART and many private and TFI Local Link services. It opens up the whole country without the cost or stress of driving. There's also an all-island scheme that covers travel in Northern Ireland.

## Choosing where to stay

- **Ground floor or lift access.** Ask when booking, especially in older country houses and B&Bs.
- **Hotels with restaurants** save going out on dark winter evenings.
- **Solo packages with dinner included** make evenings simple and sociable.
- **Midweek "golden years" offers** are common at Irish hotels, though many are priced for two; ask whether there's a single rate.

## Gentle activities

- Greenways are flat and traffic-free, and e-bikes make them easy.
- Gardens such as Mount Stewart, Powerscourt, Bantry House and Kylemore's walled garden.
- Museums, galleries and heritage towns.
- Boat trips on lakes and to islands in calm weather.

## Tours

Many coach and small-group tours are popular with older solo travellers, and some are designed specifically for people travelling alone, with no single supplement. Check the pace and the amount of walking before booking.

## Health and insurance

Bring enough of any medication for the whole trip, plus a copy of your prescription. If you're visiting from abroad, check your travel insurance covers pre-existing conditions. See our guide to [travel insurance](/guides/travel-insurance-for-solo-travellers-what-to-check).

## Staying in touch

Let family know your plans, keep your phone charged, and consider sharing your location. It's reassuring for everyone.
