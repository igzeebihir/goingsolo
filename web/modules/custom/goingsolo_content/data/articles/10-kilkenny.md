If you've never been away on your own before, Kilkenny is a gentle place to start. It's small, attractive and full of things to do, and you can walk everywhere. It's about an hour and a half from Dublin by train.

## The Medieval Mile

Kilkenny's main sights sit along a single walkable route known as the Medieval Mile. Start at **Kilkenny Castle** and its park, then walk past the Tholsel and down to **St Canice's Cathedral**, where you can climb the round tower for a view of the city. The **Medieval Mile Museum** tells the story in between.

## Craft and design

Across from the castle, the **Design Centre** in the old castle stables sells Irish craft and has a good café. Kilkenny has many small studios and galleries, and the National Design and Craft Gallery is free.

## Food and drink

Kilkenny has a strong food scene for its size, with cafés, bakeries and restaurants that are comfortable for solo diners. The **Smithwick's Experience** brewery tour is a sociable way to spend an hour.

## Beyond the city

If you have an extra day, Jerpoint Abbey, the village of Inistioge and the River Nore walks are close by, though easiest to reach by car or taxi.

## Where to stay

Visit Kilkenny recommends several hotels for solo travellers, including city-centre hotels with single occupancy rates. Look at our [Kilkenny deals](/county/kilkenny), and avoid the busy weekends of the Kilkenny Arts Festival in August and the Cat Laughs comedy festival in early June unless you're going for them.
