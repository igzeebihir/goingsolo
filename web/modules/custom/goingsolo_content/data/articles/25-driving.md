Public transport covers the main routes, but to explore Connemara, Donegal, the Beara Peninsula or the smaller corners of the country, a car helps. Driving alone takes a little more planning, because there's nobody to navigate or share the wheel.

## Renting

- **Book early** for summer, and compare the total price including insurance. Car hire insurance in Ireland is often more expensive than visitors expect, and some credit card cover isn't accepted.
- **Automatics** are less common and cost more. Reserve one if you need it.
- **Age limits** apply. Many companies have minimum ages around 25 and some charge more for drivers over 70 or 75. Check before booking.
- **Pick a smaller car.** It's cheaper and much easier on narrow roads.

## Rules of the road

- Drive on the **left**.
- Speed limits are in **kilometres per hour** in the Republic and **miles per hour** in Northern Ireland.
- The drink-driving limit is very low in the Republic. The safest approach is not to drink at all if you're driving.
- The M50 around Dublin has a barrier-free toll. Pay online by 8pm the next day, or check whether your rental company handles it.

## Rural roads

Many country roads are narrow, winding and bordered by stone walls or hedges, with no hard shoulder. Slow down, pull in to let others pass, and watch for tractors, cyclists, walkers and sheep. On roads like the Ring of Kerry, tour coaches travel anticlockwise; some drivers prefer to go the same way to avoid meeting them on tight bends.

## Driving solo

- **Plan routes in daylight**, especially in winter when it's dark by 5pm.
- **Download offline maps**, because mobile signal drops out in the mountains and on peninsulas.
- **Take breaks** every couple of hours. Long days at the wheel are tiring when you're the only driver.
- **Keep fuel topped up** in remote areas, where stations can be far apart and closed on Sundays.
- **Carry water, snacks and a phone charger** in the car.

## Mix and match

Many solo travellers take the train to a hub such as Galway, Killarney or Westport and rent a car there for a few days. That cuts cost and the stress of city driving.
